The hard part isn’t which covariate predicts response — it’s telling real signal from a lucky split

  • 500 simulated DM1 patients, 3 therapies (Mexiletine, Oligonucleotide, Tideglusib), 18 baseline covariates each.
  • One question per arm: which baseline covariates predict who responds? — asked on two endpoints, a continuous improvement score and a binary responder label.
  • The trap: on data this size, a single train/test split can crown a different “answer” every time. The real question is which signals are real versus an artifact of who landed in the test set.
  • Our claim: ask it the right way and the answer is reproducible; trust one leaderboard and it is misleading.

Same held-out patients for all 14 models — so a score gap is the method, not the sample

14 models × 2 endpoints × 3 arms · one locked pipeline
  • 14 methods per task — 8 inference (OLS/logistic, stepwise AIC, stepwise BIC, best subset, LASSO, elastic net, ridge, GAM) + 6 prediction (random forest, gradient boosting untuned/tuned, SVM, kNN, MLP).
  • Gradient boosting = scikit-learn HistGradientBoosting, filling XGBoost’s role (no OpenMP runtime here) — same job, fully reproducible (docs/LOG.md).
  • All 14 scored on the same held-out rows, so a score gap is the method, not the sample.

The honest headline: the train/test split moves the scores, not the covariates

  • Across 20 reseeded splits, the median per-method test-R² SD is 0.131 (compare/verdict.md) — any ranking inside that band is a tie, not a winner.
  • Same arm, different split, the leading R² swings: Tideglusib +0.26 / +0.30 / +0.35; Mexiletine +0.19 / −0.01 / +0.14 (fold5 / single80 / single75).
  • Yet the covariates barely move: across all 20 seeds, Tideglusib cognition 100%, Oligonucleotide CTG repeats 90%, Mexiletine myotonia 85%.
  • So we never crown a model. We identify the covariate signal that survives resampling — that is the reproducible object.

A shuffled-outcome null plus stability selection separates real signal from a field of noise

  • Pre-registered bar, fixed before scoring: a covariate is “real signal” only if stable in ≥60% of 100 half-subsamples AND beats the 95th percentile of a shuffled-outcome RF null (docs/hypothesis_sheet.md).
  • Replicating real signals: Tideglusib cognition (both endpoints) and Oligonucleotide CTG repeats (both endpoints).
  • Honesty on display: Mexiletine’s severity trio (myotonia, CTG, duration) is stable but inside the RF null on the relative target → “suggestive,” not real (slide_tables.md).
  • A flag we kept, not hid: Oligo insulin_resistance met the real-signal bar on fold 5 (stable 0.78, beats the null 0.262 > 0.172) — but we override it: it holds in only 17% of cross-seed splits (and q = 0.075 under FDR). Downgraded, not claimed.

Tideglusib is the showcase: ML found a cognition threshold, a one-term rule captures it, and biology explains it

  • ML found it: random forest’s 20-seed mean R² = 0.491 vs ~0.29 (OLS) – 0.36 (LASSO) for the linear models (compare/repeat20_r2.csv) — a nonlinearity linear fits miss.
  • It’s a step, not a slope: partial dependence holds a high plateau (~8) for low baseline cognition, then cliffs ~4 points to a ~3.8 floor and flattens — a spline GAM (R² 0.119) underfits it.
  • One term captures it: the data-chosen cut 1[cognitive_function ≤ −0.505] lifts single-term test R² from +0.233 (linear) to +0.377 (indicator) (rule.txt) — one held-out fold (~34 pts), so it corroborates the 20-seed + cross-seed evidence; re-estimated on fold 1.
  • Biology explains it: a cognition-weighted composite + GSK-3β target → a head-room/floor effect. In-sample (48/102) the rule enriches mean improvement 3.46 → 8.28 (2.4×), keeping ~47% — but with no placebo arm this is on-treatment response, i.e. prognostic enrichment to confirm against a control, not yet separable from regression-to-the-mean / floor. Marginal ρ −0.647 (q = 4e-16; outcome_associations.csv); the pre-registered step near z ≈ −0.5 landed on −0.505.

Both other arms, told honestly: the label/axis is real; the per-patient magnitude isn’t

Mexiletine — label yes, magnitude no

  • The responder label is cleanly separable (stepwise logistic ROC-AUC 0.773, CI 0.587–0.928), even though improvement magnitude is not (best R² ≈ 0.19, CI through 0). The relative target divides severity back out.
  • Enrichment (prognostic): CTG > 448 ~doubles responder prevalence, 0.289 → 0.543 — an in-sample descriptive stat over folds 2–5 (N=128; enriched n=35, wide CI), from the raw dataset (interpretation_Mexiletine.md §6), not held-out scoring.

Oligonucleotide — axis yes, prediction no

  • CTG repeats is a real, stable axis (90% on the continuous endpoint — half-subsample + cross-seed; 76% binary) but per-patient QMT change is unpredictable out-of-sample (R² 0.057, CI [−0.29, +0.25]). Use CTG to stratify, not predict individuals.
  • Flag for the locked run: repeat-burden direction is positive here (ρ +0.42, q = 6e-6; outcome_associations.csv), opposite the mechanistic ceiling prior — pre-specify the sign, confirm on fold 1.
