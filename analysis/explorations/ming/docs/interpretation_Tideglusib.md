# Tideglusib — covariate interpretation

**Target:** continuous = `tideglusib_composite` (combined cognitive + motor improvement at Week 26, 0–20, higher = better); binary = responder label.
**Scoring status (rehearsal):** every number below is from the **fold-5 pseudo-test**. The locked **fold-1 test set has not been opened** and is not scored here. The fold-5 Tideglusib test set is small (~33 patients), so bootstrap CIs are wide and single-fold rankings inside the ~0.131 R² tie band (`outputs/compare/verdict.md`) are treated as ties.

## 1. Headline
Baseline cognition is the entire tideglusib story, and it is a **step, not a slope**: a single data-chosen threshold `1[cognitive_function ≤ −0.505]` recovers almost all of the random forest's nonlinear gain — indicator test **R² = +0.377** versus **+0.233** for a linear cognition term (`outputs/stratify/Tideglusib/rule.txt`) — and splits Week-26 composite improvement into a high-response group (mean **8.277**) and a low-response group (mean **3.457**) (`rule.txt`). Cognition is the only covariate flagged **"real signal" in both** the continuous and binary tasks and is selected in **100%** of cross-seed fits (`outputs/compare/selection_frequency.csv`, 60/60).

## 2. Mechanism
Tideglusib is a **GSK-3β inhibitor**, and in DM1 the composite endpoint is **cognition-weighted** (combined cognitive + motor; `work/dictionary.json`). `cognitive_function` is a baseline z-score versus the population mean, so negative values mean below-average cognition. The marginal association is the strongest of any covariate in any arm: Spearman rho = **−0.647** (95% CI −0.724 to −0.550, q = 4.25e-16; `results/tables/outcome_associations.csv`) — **lower baseline cognition → larger composite improvement**. This is a textbook **floor/head-room effect**: patients already below a cognitive-impairment threshold have the most room to gain on the cognition-weighted composite, while patients at or above population-average cognition have little head-room and plateau. A *threshold* (rather than a smooth gradient) is biologically sensible — response unlocks once baseline cognition is impaired enough to move — and that is exactly what the data show.

**Reconciliation with the pre-registration:** the hypothesis sheet pre-registered a "threshold/step near z ≈ −0.5" for `cognitive_function` (`docs/hypothesis_sheet.md`). The depth-1 tree, choosing its own cut from the data, returned **−0.505** (`rule.txt`) — the prediction landed essentially on the nose.

## 3. The arc: linear → forest → one-term rule
1. **Linear tier (fold-5, `outputs/runs/continuous/fold5/Tideglusib/scoreboard.csv`):** the full OLS over-fits (R² = **−0.011**); regularized/stepwise linear models top out at R² = **0.170** (best subset / stepwise BIC); and a spline **GAM** — which *can* bend — reaches only R² = **0.119**. A deliberately compact **one-term** linear model on cognition does best of all the linear fits at R² = **0.233** (`outputs/runs/continuous/fold5/Tideglusib/compact_scoreboard.csv`); adding `disease_duration` moves it only +0.010 (to 0.243).
2. **Forest:** random forest reaches R² = **0.255** on fold-5 (`scoreboard.csv`; CI −0.399 to 0.606), and — the robust evidence — the **highest 20-seed mean R² = 0.491** of the repeated methods, versus 0.372 (gradient boosting untuned), 0.363 (LASSO), 0.330 (ridge), 0.288 (OLS) (`outputs/compare/repeat20_r2.csv`). Leading R² stays positive and large across all three split schemes (fold5 +0.26, single80 +0.30, single75 +0.35; `verdict.md`).
3. **One-term rule recovers the nonlinearity:** the single hard indicator `1[cognitive_function ≤ −0.505]` scores test **R² = +0.377**, beating the single *linear* cognition term (+0.233) by **+0.144** (`rule.txt`). That a hard step beats both the linear term *and* the spline GAM (0.119) is the tell: the effect is a **discontinuity**, which splines underfit but one threshold captures.

**Partial-dependence shape (`outputs/stratify/Tideglusib/partial_dependence.csv`):** the forest's PD is a near-flat **high plateau (~8.1–9.3)** for cognition below ~−0.56, then **cliffs by ~3.67 points** (8.10 → 4.43) across the −0.56 → −0.48 interval, and settles onto a flat **low plateau (~3.8)** above. The "bend" the prior anticipated is in fact a step centered at the −0.505 cut.

## 4. Real signal vs noise
By the pre-registered rule (stable at ≥0.6 **and** RF importance above the 95th-percentile shuffled null), `cognitive_function` is **"real signal" in both tasks**: continuous stability freq 1.0 with RF importance **1.270 > null 0.177** (`outputs/stability/continuous/Tideglusib/stability.csv`); binary stability freq 1.0, beats null (`outputs/stability/binary/Tideglusib/stability.csv`). It is the only covariate real-signal in both. In continuous, `disease_duration` (freq 0.97) and the rest are merely **"suggestive"** (importance below null); `muscle_strength` is **"noise"**. In binary, `myotonia_severity` and `somatic_mosaicism_index` also clear a *near-zero* null and read "real signal," but their importances (3.85e-5, 4.8e-4) are negligible beside cognition's 0.089 — cognition is the dominant binary signal too (single-term logistic AUC **0.731**, the top binary score; `.../binary/.../compact_scoreboard.csv`). Cross-seed, cognition is selected **100%** (60/60), with `disease_duration` next at 93% (`selection_frequency.csv`).

## 5. Enrichment rule
**Rule:** `1[cognitive_function ≤ −0.505]` (`rule.txt`).
- **Subgroup size:** 48 of 102 training patients (**~47%**) — roughly half the screened population, not a narrow slice.
- **Enriched vs other mean composite improvement:** **8.277 vs 3.457** — a **+4.82-point (2.4×)** gap on the 0–20 scale.
- **Decision relevance:** the cut is a single, pre-specifiable baseline measurement that nearly doubles mean on-treatment response while retaining ~half of candidates — a practical inclusion criterion, not an exotic multivariate score.

## 7. Trial-design implication
Enriching enrollment on a pre-specified **baseline cognition z ≤ −0.5** would retain ~47% of candidates (`rule.txt`) while raising mean composite response from **3.46 to 8.28** (a 2.4× concentration of signal). Because required N scales with the inverse square of the standardized effect, even a partial carry-over of this response gap into a controlled treatment-vs-placebo contrast would materially cut enrollment; a fully preserved 2.4× effect is an illustrative **ceiling of ~5–6× fewer patients** for the same power. This is a response-enrichment and head-room argument, **hedged on three fronts**: (a) this dataset has no placebo arm — the 8.28 vs 3.46 gap is on-treatment response, not an isolated treatment effect; (b) the fold-5 test is small (~33 patients) with wide CIs; and (c) the **locked fold-1 confirmation has not been run**. The pre-specified next step is to re-estimate the −0.505 cut and its enrichment on fold-1 once the gate opens, after confirming the fold-1 hash.
