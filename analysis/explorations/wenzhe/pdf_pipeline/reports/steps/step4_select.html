4.2 Decision

  • Small n (128-136) and p of 19-20: n/p is about 7 and the label has only 1.9 to 3.2 responders per column, so unpenalised or flexible models will overfit. The covariate track therefore leads with penalised regression (ridge, LASSO, elastic net), BIC stepwise selection, best subset and a GAM, with lm/glm as the reference. The ML track uses only small tuning grids, because a large grid on a small arm mostly finds noise.
  • Correlated covariates: log10 CTG and myotonia severity correlate at 0.84-0.90 in every arm. LASSO may arbitrarily drop one of a correlated pair, so ridge/elastic net, PCR and several selectors are run side by side and agreement across methods is read as evidence (Step 6.5).
  • Both tracks run in parallel on identical folds and identical test patients. The covariate track answers 'which covariates matter and how'; the ML track is the predictive comparison that answers 'can ML improve beyond covariate modelling?'.
  • Outcome: the continuous outcome is primary (metrics RMSE, MAE, R2); the responder label is the parallel classification task (ROC-AUC, recall, precision, accuracy at a stated cutoff).
